They are the power-ups that fall down the screen in a 1997 arcade shooter: tiny icons of
sixteen by sixteen pixels. Each row holds, from left to right and carrying on in the next
row: a glass vacuum tube with an orange filament on a brown base; a red and yellow toy ray
gun; a silver LaserDisc with a rainbow wedge; a dark grey boom box with two round speakers
and a handle; a slice of pepperoni pizza; a blue soap-bubble wand with a pale bubble in its
ring; a black CB radio handset with a coiled cord; a silver mirror ball; a black VHS
cassette with two white reel windows and a white label; a coral-red can of hairspray with a
white cap; a red horseshoe magnet with silver tips; a silver coin with a ridged rim and no
face on it; a taller vacuum valve on a brass socket, LIT, glowing orange and yellow inside;
and the same valve UNLIT, the same outline, its glass dark blue-grey, the filament cold
grey. The dark things (the handset, the cassette, the boom box) are drawn in blue-grey with
light edges so that their shapes read, not as black blobs.
