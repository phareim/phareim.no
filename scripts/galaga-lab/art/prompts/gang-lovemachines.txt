They are one gang of enemies in a 1997 vertical-scrolling arcade shooter: THE LOVE MACHINES,
the toys and furniture of a 1968 bachelor pad gone wrong. Their colours: tangerine orange,
mustard yellow, avocado green, turquoise, cream and chocolate brown, with black-and-white
op-art stripes. No pink and no purple anywhere. {above} {down}
Top row, left to right: 1. the grunt: a lava-lamp blob, a small cone-shaped lava lamp flying
with its chrome cap pointing down, round orange blobs floating in yellow liquid behind the
glass, a chrome base at the top. 2. the flanker: a feathered glider, a wide flat glider
whose two wings are fans of long turquoise and green feathers, a small cream body in the
middle, a yellow beak pointing down. 3. the tank, the largest and widest: an armoured
flower, a round chocolate-brown core ringed by six big riveted orange petal plates, wide and
heavy.
Middle row, left to right: 4. the diver: a dart, one long arrow pointing straight down with
a red heart-shaped head at the bottom and cream feathers at the top. 5. the sniper: an
eyeball in a ring of black-and-white op-art stripes, with one very long thin kaleidoscope
tube barrel pointing straight down from it, far longer than the eye. 6. the splitter:
visibly two halves, a set of wind-up chattering teeth held open: an upper jaw and a lower
jaw of big white teeth in red gums, joined only by a small brass hinge, with a brass wind-up
key at the side.
Bottom row, left to right: 7. the mite, the smallest, tiny: one small round orange lava blob
with a single eye. 8. the captor, large: a big round hypnotic dish of black-and-white op-art
rings, a spiral, with one turquoise eye in its middle, on a small cream body with two short
fins. 9. the raider: a tin wind-up doll with a round cream head, a brass key in its back at
the top and two long jointed arms reaching down, each ending in an open pincer hand.
