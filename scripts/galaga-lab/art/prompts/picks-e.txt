They are the power-ups that fall down the screen in a 1997 arcade shooter: tiny icons of
sixteen by sixteen pixels, twelve columns of them, every column the same thing all the way
down, nine rows. The columns, from left to right: 1. a glass vacuum tube with an orange
filament on a brown base. 2. a red and yellow toy ray gun. 3. a silver LaserDisc with a
rainbow wedge. 4. a blue-grey boom box with two round speakers and a handle. 5. a slice of
pepperoni pizza. 6. a soap-bubble wand: a yellow plastic ring on a short yellow stick, and
filling the ring one round sky-blue soap bubble with a white glint (solid sky blue, not
see-through). 7. a CB radio microphone: a chunky cream-coloured bakelite hand microphone
with a dark grille of slots and a red button, a short curly brown cord from its lower end.
8. a mirror ball: a round silver disco ball of square facets, some facets white, some
blue-grey, a small cap on top. 9. a VHS video cassette: a wide flat blue-grey box with two
round white reel windows and a white label strip above them. 10. a coral-red can of
hairspray with a white cap. 11. a red horseshoe magnet with silver tips. 12. a silver coin
with a ridged rim and a small star stamped in the middle.
Under them, two more rows, taller: vacuum valves on brass sockets for an instrument panel,
by turns one LIT (glowing orange and yellow inside) and one UNLIT (the same outline, the
glass dark blue-grey, the filament cold grey), lit, unlit, lit, unlit along the row.
Every icon keeps to its own column; no two are merged; none is left out.
